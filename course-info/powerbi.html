What learners will be able to do

From the Power BI Desktop interface and data import through modelling, DAX, interactive reports, publishing and presentations.

  • Recognise the Report, Data and Model views, main ribbon and essential panes without prior Power BI experience.
  • Import spreadsheets or CSVs and identify incorrect types, missing fields and quality problems before modelling.
  • Model sales facts with reusable dimension tables to prevent misleading totals and filters.

Is this course right for you?

Worked example and explanation (open after trying)
Business example: A retailer wants a dashboard that can filter by month, store and product without double-counting sales. Worked solution or syntax:

DimDate[Date] (1) → (*) FactSales[OrderDate]

Each fact row must match at most one product dimension key; a filtered total should never increase from an accidental join.

Using a calculated column to replace a dynamic KPI measure or dividing without checking zeros.

Worked example and explanation (open after trying)
Business example: Management wants accurate performance metrics that adjust instantly to the selected region and period. Worked solution or syntax:
Sales Revenue = SUM(FactSales[Revenue])
Orders = DISTINCTCOUNT(FactSales[OrderID])
AOV = DIVIDE([Sales Revenue],[Orders])

AOV must equal selected revenue divided by selected distinct orders, not average of precomputed category averages.

Success criteria (practice guidance, not a grading rubric): Produce the requested output, explain how it was obtained, check it against the example and identify one possible error or limitation.

Hint: where to start

Comparing a partial current month with a complete previous month without explaining the limitation.

Worked example and explanation (open after trying)
Business example: A reporting team needs a credible monthly trend and variance against the previous trading period. Worked solution or syntax:
Previous Month Revenue = CALCULATE([Sales Revenue],DATEADD(DimDate[Date],-1,MONTH))
MoM % = DIVIDE([Sales Revenue]-[Previous Month Revenue],[Previous Month Revenue])

For March 135000 versus February 150000, MoM = -10%; date labels must sort chronologically.

Hint: where to start

Removing all filters unintentionally with ALL(FactSales) or treating every percentage as additive.

Worked example and explanation (open after trying)
Business example: Executives want market contribution and ranking metrics under controlled country and period slicers. Worked solution or syntax:
Region Share % = DIVIDE([Sales Revenue],CALCULATE([Sales Revenue],ALL(DimRegion)))
Rank = RANKX(ALL(DimRegion[Region]),[Sales Revenue],,DESC)

All region shares should sum to 100% when the denominator uses the appropriate total; compare to a source-based cross-check.

FAQs

Are weekly exercises graded?

The syllabus identifies guided practice and self-check questions. Formal grading, retries and pass thresholds have not been verified; instructor capstone decisions are separate from weekly completion.

What if I cannot install the required software?

Review the listed tools before starting. Equivalent tools may not support the same features; contact the course owner for an approved alternative rather than assuming compatibility.

Can I browse without an account?

Yes. Course outlines are public; interactive lessons and saved progress require sign-in.

Does completing a lesson earn a certificate?

No certificate is promised by lesson completion. Eligibility and instructor-assessed requirements must be confirmed.
