Hint: where to start

Typing data over a formula or confusing the active sheet with the entire workbook.

Worked example and explanation (open after trying)
Business example: A shop assistant needs a tidy daily sales workbook that a supervisor can reopen and understand without verbal instructions. Worked solution or syntax:
A1: Product  B1: Units  C1: Price
A2: Notebook B2: 3 C2: 1500
D2: =B2*C2  → 4500

The formula =B2*C2 multiplies 3 by 1500 to return 4500; the source values remain independently readable.

Success criteria (practice guidance, not a grading rubric): Produce the requested output, explain how it was obtained, check it against the example and identify one possible error or limitation.

Hint: where to start

Hardcoding a tax percentage in every row or confusing gross and net amounts.

Worked example and explanation (open after trying)
Business example: A finance assistant needs a dependable daily revenue sheet that recalculates when transactions change. Worked solution or syntax:
D2 = B2*C2
E2 = D2*$H$2
=SUM(D2:D101) and =AVERAGE(D2:D101)

A 3-unit order at 1500 produces 4500 before tax; a fixed 7.5% rate in H2 produces 337.50 tax.

Business example: An operations team exports inconsistent region spellings and occasional missing order dates from a point-of-sale device. Worked solution or syntax:

A label of ' north ' becomes 'North'; missing dates remain flagged rather than silently fabricated.

Week 5: IF, SUMIFS, COUNTIFS and lookup functions

Learning outcome: Calculate business KPIs by conditions and retrieve attributes from a reference table.

Estimated time: Not verified.

Hands-on task · practice: Calculate paid revenue by region, high-value orders and missing product mappings, then explain why the filters matter.

Activity type: Practice (completion requirement not verified); knowledge check is a self-check. Assessment status is not confirmed.

Success criteria (practice guidance, not a grading rubric): Produce the requested output, explain how it was obtained, check it against the example and identify one possible error or limitation.

Hint: where to start

Using a lookup on a nonunique key or letting cancelled orders inflate actual revenue.

Worked example and explanation (open after trying)
Business example: A regional manager wants comparable sales totals and product categories for a weekly trading review. Worked solution or syntax:
=IF(E2>=50000,"High","Standard")
=SUMIFS(tblSales[Revenue],tblSales[Region],"North",tblSales[Status],"Paid")
=XLOOKUP(A2,Products[ID],Products[Category],"Unmapped")

A missing ProductID returns 'Unmapped' rather than a misleading blank; paid revenue excludes cancelled sales.

FAQs

The syllabus identifies guided practice and self-check questions. Formal grading, retries and pass thresholds have not been verified; instructor capstone decisions are separate from weekly completion.

What if I cannot install the required software?

Review the listed tools before starting. Equivalent tools may not support the same features; contact the course owner for an approved alternative rather than assuming compatibility.

Yes. Course outlines are public; interactive lessons and saved progress require sign-in.

No certificate is promised by lesson completion. Eligibility and instructor-assessed requirements must be confirmed.
