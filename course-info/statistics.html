What learners will be able to do

From opening practical analysis tools through statistics, uncertainty, experiments, charts and defensible management conclusions.

  • Understand how Excel's Analysis ToolPak or Python notebooks support practical statistical questions.
  • Calculate central tendency and explain what outliers do to the story.
  • Describe volatility alongside an average rather than hiding unstable results.

Week 9: Hypothesis testing, experiments and result interpretation

Learning outcome: Use clear hypotheses and effect sizes to decide whether an apparent change is meaningful.

Estimated time: Not verified.

Hands-on task · practice: Write a one-paragraph interpretation of a test result including effect size and limitations.

Activity type: Practice (completion requirement not verified); knowledge check is a self-check. Assessment status is not confirmed.

Success criteria (practice guidance, not a grading rubric): Produce the requested output, explain how it was obtained, check it against the example and identify one possible error or limitation.

Hint: where to start

Interpreting p=0.03 as a 97% probability that the alternative hypothesis is true.

Worked example and explanation (open after trying)
Business example: A digital product team wants evidence before rolling out a new checkout design. Worked solution or syntax:
H0: new checkout has equal conversion to old; p = 0.03 at alpha = 0.05

At pre-specified alpha 0.05, reject H0 when p=0.03, subject to assumptions; do not call it proof of causation without design.
