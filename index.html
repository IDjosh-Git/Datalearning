A LEARNING PATH, NOT A LINK DIRECTORY

Know what you're working towards, from day one.

Courses are only one part of becoming a capable analyst. DataLearning connects each concept to a task, a checked result, and a project you can explain. The six detailed course outlines live in the course catalogue—there's no need to repeat them here.

01
UNDERSTAND

Start with the business question

Identify who needs an answer, what decision they are making, and which measures would help. Work through examples before opening a tool.

Example: Why did revenue change between two periods?

02
PRACTISE

Work with real analytical steps

Clean a sample, calculate a metric, write a query, build a visual, or test a Python approach. Retry when a result is wrong rather than moving on blindly.

Example: Validate monthly totals before reporting growth.

03
EXPLAIN

Prove you understand the result

Use short quizzes, active recall and your own explanation. Record uncertainty, assumptions and data-quality limits so you can justify the outcome.

Example: Explain a variance in plain language.

04
BUILD

Turn skills into a portfolio project

Bring techniques together in a capstone: prepare data, document the method, show the evidence and communicate a decision. Instructor review is separate from self-study.

Example: Present a defensible sales-performance report.

GOOD TO KNOW

Answers before you begin

Clear expectations help you plan a realistic learning routine.

Do I need to sign in to explore lessons?

No. Public courses and the free lesson preview can be explored without creating an account. Sign in when you need verified learning records and account-specific features.

How do I know which course to start with?

Use the goal selector above or answer the four questions in the starting-point guide. Foundations are suitable for complete beginners; the full course catalogue explains prerequisites and outcomes for each specialist path.

What happens when I get a practice question wrong?

Review the expected output, identify the mistake, read the relevant example and retry. A correction and explanation matter more than the number of attempts.

Will completing a lesson automatically award a certificate?

No. Course lesson completion, practice history, quizzes and assessed capstone requirements are separate. An instructor must evaluate the required project evidence before an eligible certificate is issued.
